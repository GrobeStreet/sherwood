The whole game is rigged. So we flipped it.

Here's the secret the people charging you don't want out: most of it was never theirs to sell. The VIN history. The flood map. The recall notice. The wage figures. The hull records. Public data — gathered with public money, sitting on government servers, already yours. Then somebody bolts on a login, prints a price, and sells you back what you already own.

Sherwood started with one person who got sick of it — a solo builder quoted thousands for a website, bled dry by a CRM, renting tools just to post online, slowly realizing the little guy wasn't meant to win. Not alone. Not against companies with teams and budgets and a hundred ways to charge you.

So they stopped trying to buy their way in and learned to build. Not to replace anyone — to orchestrate. To stand up the things the gatekeepers swore took a department and a down payment. And once the first paywall fell, the rest looked flimsy too. Every "premium report" was free underneath. Every "pro tool" was a padlock on a public door.

They used their position to lock up what belongs to everyone. We're just handing out the key.

So that's what this is. Free apps that do the thing the paid ones do — without the account, the email, the "we just need a few details." You show up, you get what you came for, you leave a little less ripped off. No name on the door, no pitch, no upsell waiting three screens in. Just the stuff, free.

It's no fun for them when the rabbit's got the gun.
